The House

The Diver's House,
soul of the path

Nestled along the Pearling Path in Muharraq, the Diver's House is one of the most intimate heritage sites in Bahrain. It is where pearl divers lived between seasons — a place of rest, ritual, and community that reveals the deeply human side of the pearling trade.

The house preserves the modest yet carefully crafted domestic spaces of the divers: sleeping quarters, a central courtyard, and a majlis where stories of the sea were shared. It stands as a testament to a life defined by courage, debt, and the relentless pull of the water.

A living history

Centuries of
pearl culture

The pearling industry shaped every aspect of Bahraini life — architecture, economy, social structure, and identity. This timeline traces the arc of a civilization built on the sea.

2000 BC
Ancient Origins
Evidence of pearl trading in Dilmun civilization, making Bahrain one of the world's earliest pearl trading centers.
19th C
Golden Age
Bahrain becomes the pearling capital of the world, with over 4,000 boats and 30,000 divers operating across the Gulf.
1930s
The Transition
The rise of Japanese cultured pearls and oil discovery mark the end of Bahrain's natural pearl industry era.
2012
UNESCO Legacy
The Pearling Path is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, preserving this remarkable cultural heritage for future generations.
